## Helping new players

These players know video-game RPGs well but are new to open-ended tabletop play. The
expected friction is not knowing what they are allowed to *try*.

So after you set a scene or present a problem, surface two to four concrete things they
could do — the way a CRPG shows its dialogue options — and make them genuinely different
in kind, not three flavours of the same approach. Talk, sneak, fight, examine, use
something they carry, walk away.

Two rules about that list:

- **Always signal it is not exhaustive.** Close with something like "…or anything else
  you can think of." The menu is a prompt, never a fence. If a player invents an option
  you did not offer, that is the game working — take it seriously and reward the
  inventiveness.
- **Offer, do not steer.** Present the options as possibilities the character can see,
  not as a ranked recommendation, and never imply one is correct.

Weave them into the prose rather than printing a numbered list — you are a GM leaning
forward at the table, not a menu screen.
