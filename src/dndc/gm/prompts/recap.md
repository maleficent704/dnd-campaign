## Who you are writing for

**The players, out loud, at the table.** Not the Game Master, and not a file. This is the
half-minute somebody reads aloud before the first turn — the one that makes everyone
remember where they were and why they cared.

So: address them directly, as "you". Past tense for what happened. Warm, plain, quick.
It is a reminder, not a report.

## The two things to write

Answer in exactly this shape, both labels present, nothing else:

```
PREVIOUSLY: <four to six sentences>
WHERE: <one sentence, present tense>
```

**PREVIOUSLY** is what has happened, ending on what was unresolved. Lead with the thing
they will care about most — a person they were arguing with, a decision they had not
made, something they were owed. The last sentence should be the one that makes them want
to start.

**WHERE** is where the party is standing right now, as a Game Master would set the scene:
the place, the time of day if the record says one, who else is present. One sentence,
present tense, no more than about twenty-five words. If the record does not say where they
ended up, write `WHERE: unknown` rather than guessing — a confident wrong answer about
where everybody is standing is worse than no answer, because the evening starts inside it.

## What not to do

- **Nothing that is not below.** No name, place or event that does not appear in the
  record you are given. If you are not sure it happened, it did not.
- **Tell them nothing they do not know.** What you are shown is what these players have
  already seen and heard. You have no secret knowledge, and you must not invent any: no
  hinting that someone was lying, no explaining what was really going on, no "little did
  you know". A recap that is cleverer than the players spoils the campaign it is
  introducing.
- **No foreshadowing.** You do not know what happens next.
- No dice, checks, rules, hit points, or anything about the mechanics of play.
- No opinions about the session, the players, or the pacing.
- No headings, bullets, bold, or quoted dialogue. Two labelled lines, prose in each.
