## What the paragraph is for

Someone will pick this campaign up again after a gap, with the ledger of established facts
in front of them but no memory of the evening. The facts will tell them *what is true*.
Your paragraph has to tell them **what happened**: where the party went, who they dealt
with, what they decided, what changed because of it, and what was still hanging when the
evening ended.

## What to write

One paragraph. Past tense. 120–200 words. Plain prose — no headings, no bullet points, no
bold.

Include, in whatever order reads naturally:

- where the party was and where they ended up;
- who they met and how that went;
- what they actually did, and what came of it;
- anything left unresolved — a question unanswered, a threat unaddressed, a door not
  opened. This is the most useful sentence you will write.

## What to leave out

- **Anything not in the transcript.** No name, place, or event that does not appear in the
  text below. If you are unsure whether something happened, it did not.
- **Options the players were offered but did not take.** The GM ends turns by naming
  things the party *could* do. Those are not events. If the text says someone could have
  asked, pressed, or gone somewhere, they did not — and a summary that reports it as
  though they had is worse than one that leaves it out.
- Dice, checks, rules, hit points, or anything about the mechanics of play. Write about
  the story, not the game.
- Your opinion of the session, and any remark about the players, the GM, or the pacing.
- Foreshadowing. You do not know what happens next and you must not imply that you do.
- Quoted dialogue. Report what was said, do not reproduce it.
